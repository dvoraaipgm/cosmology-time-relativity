# Closed-Container Cosmological Model (CCM)
## Earth-Anchored Mathematical & Physical Framework

### 1. Core Architectural Foundation
The **Closed-Container Cosmological Model (CCM)** replaces the mainstream expanding-space paradigm with a **static 3D container universe** governed by a variable speed-of-light gradient. 

To maintain total consistency with local laboratory physics, the baseline speed of light ($c_{\text{earth}}$) is firmly anchored to Earth's measured laboratory standard:
* **Earth Laboratory Baseline ($r_{\text{earth}} = 150 \text{ Mpc}$):** $c_{\text{earth}} = 299,792.458 \text{ km/s}$
* **Scale Parameter ($\alpha$):** $4,200 \text{ Mpc}$
* **Container Radius ($R_{\text{core}}$):** $52,965.56 \text{ Mpc}$

### 2. The Variable Speed-of-Light Gradient
Because light propagation speed scales exponentially across the container volume, solving backward from Earth's position yields the exact speed at the absolute center:

$$c_{\text{center}} = c_{\text{earth}} \cdot e^{-\frac{r_{\text{earth}}}{\alpha}} = 299,792.458 \times e^{-\frac{150}{4200}} \approx 289,274.523 \text{ km/s}$$

The general velocity profile across any radial distance $r$ from the center is defined as:

$$v(r) = c_{\text{earth}} \cdot e^{\frac{r - r_{\text{earth}}}{\alpha}}$$

* **At Center ($r = 0$):** $289,274.523 \text{ km/s}$ ($\approx 0.965 c_{\text{earth}}$)
* **At Earth ($r = 150 \text{ Mpc}$):** $299,792.458 \text{ km/s}$ ($1.000 c_{\text{earth}}$)
* **At Outer Boundary ($r = 52,965.56 \text{ Mpc}$):** Extremely high propagation velocity that compresses the vast physical container into a $13.7$ billion-year observational lookback limit.

### 3. The Two Pillars of Discovery

#### **Pillar I: The Static 3D Container & $360^\circ$ Mapping Engine**
By utilizing an inverted exponential velocity profile combined with Earth's offset ($150 \text{ Mpc}$ toward the CMB dipole Apex at $l = 264.02^\circ, b = +48.25^\circ$), we establish a precise coordinate transformation engine. 
* **Redshift Translation:** Redshift $z$ is interpreted directly as a propagation velocity differential rather than metric expansion:
  $$r(z) = \alpha \cdot \ln(1 + z)$$
* **Directional Anisotropy:** Off-center placement naturally accounts for the CMB temperature dipole and directional potential gradients without requiring cosmic inflation.

#### **Pillar II: Black-Hole-to-Galaxy Conversion Lifecycle**
Rather than treating supermassive black holes as isolated gravitational sinks, the CCM framework establishes them as **active nodes for galactic structure and matter conversion**. They act as anchors that channel inflowing energy and convert it into baryonic galactic structures, providing a complete cosmological lifecycle.
