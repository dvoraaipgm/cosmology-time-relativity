1. Resolution of the Hubble Tension

The Hubble Tension—the systemic discrepancy between early-universe CMB measurements (~67.4 km/s/Mpc) and local distance-ladder measurements (~73–74 km/s/Mpc)—is naturally resolved under the CCM framework. Rather than requiring dark energy driving an accelerated spatial expansion, redshift is governed by gravitational energy loss as light climbs out of varying cosmic potential gradients within a bounded spherical container.

Because local measurements are taken across regions of higher localized potential gradients, photons experience localized gravitational climbing resistance, yielding higher apparent recession rates ($H_0$). The CMB measurements reach equilibrium near the boundary zone, naturally producing the lower measured baseline. Adjusting for gradient-based climbing removes the tension completely without requiring dark energy.
